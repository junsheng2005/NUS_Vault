# [[ME1103 - Review on Force and Moment.pdf#page=20&selection=0,31,0,32|Moment of a Force about a Point]]

The moment of a force is the vector product of $\vec{r}$ and $\vec{F}$. The direction of $\vec{M_{O}}$ must be perpendicular to the plane containing $\vec{r}$ and $\vec{F}$, which follows the right hand screw rule.
$$
	\vec{M_{O}}=\vec{r}\times \vec{F}
$$

## [[ME1103 - Review on Force and Moment.pdf#page=21&selection=0,14,0,15|Vector Product]]
$$\begin{aligned} \vec{V} = \vec{P} \times \vec{Q} &= \begin{vmatrix} \vec{\imath} & \vec{\jmath} & \vec{k} \\ P_x & P_y & P_z \\ Q_x & Q_y & Q_z \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} P_y & P_z \\ Q_y & Q_z \end{vmatrix} \vec{\imath} - \begin{vmatrix} P_x & P_z \\ Q_x & Q_z \end{vmatrix} \vec{\jmath} + \begin{vmatrix} P_x & P_y \\ Q_x & Q_y \end{vmatrix} \vec{k} \\ &= (P_y Q_z - P_z Q_y)\vec{\imath} + (P_z Q_x - P_x Q_z)\vec{\jmath} + (P_x Q_y - P_y Q_x)\vec{k} \end{aligned}$$
## [[ME1103 - Review on Force and Moment.pdf#page=23&selection=0,23,0,24|Moment of a Force in 2D]]

In 2D planar, where $\vec{r}=r_{x}\vec{i}+r_{y}\vec{j}$, and $\vec{F}=F_{x}\vec{i}+F_{y}\vec{j}$  and $r_{z}=F_{z}=0$ , the moment if reduced to:
$$
	\vec{M_{O}}=(r_{x}F_{y}-r_{y}F_{x})\vec{k}

$$
Positive value implies rotational effect in the counter clockwise direction
### Magnitude Formula

$$
	M_{O}=\|\vec{r}\|\|\vec{F}\|\sin\theta
$$
Direction is determined by right hand screw rule.

# [[ME1103 - Review on Force and Moment.pdf#page=32&selection=0,18,0,19|Moment of a Couple]]

A couple is formed when two forces $\vec{F}$ and $-\vec{F}$ having the same magnitude, with parallel lines of action, but opposite sense.

Couple only produces pure rotational effect on a body.
$$
	\vec{M}=\vec{r_{A}}\times \vec{F}+\vec{r_{B}}\times -\vec{F}=(\vec{r_{A}}-\vec{r_{B}})\times \vec{F}=\vec{r_{A/B}}\times \vec{F}

$$
where its magnitude is:
$$
	M=rF\sin\theta=Fd
$$
and $d$ is the shortest distance between the pair of forces.
![](./Attachments/1.%20Review%20on%20Force%20and%20Moment-1786677763659.webp)![](./Attachments/1.%20Review%20on%20Force%20and%20Moment-1786677843634.webp)
Two couples are identical if:
- They have the same magnitude
- The two couples lie in parallel planes
- The two couples have the same sense of the tendency to cause rotation in the same direction
