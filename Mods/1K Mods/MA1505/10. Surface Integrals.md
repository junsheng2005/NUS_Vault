# [[Parametric Surfaces]]

## General

A parametric representation of a surface is given by the two variable vector function
$$
\textbf{r}(u,v)=x(u,v)\textbf{i}+y(u,v)\textbf{j}+z(u,v)\textbf{k}
$$

To obtain the parametric equation from a linear equation,

$\text{If}\ z=f(x,y)$

$\text{Let}\ x(u,v)=u,y(u,v)=v,$

$\text{Then}\ z=f(x(u,v),y(u,v))=f(u,v)$

$\therefore \textbf{r}(u,v)=u\textbf{i}+v\textbf{j}+f(u,v)\textbf{k}$

## Sphere

Standard parametric representation for a sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2=a^2$ is
$$
\textbf{r}(u,v)
=(a\sin{u}\cos{v}) \textbf{i}+
(a\sin{u}\cos{v}) \textbf{j}+
(a\cos{u})\textbf{k}
$$
### Limits for Full Sphere
$$0\le u \le \pi,0 \le v \le 2\pi$$
### Limits for Upper Hemisphere

$$
0\le u \le \frac{\pi}{2},0 \le v \le 2\pi
$$
## Cylinder

Standard parametric representation of a cylinder $x^2+y^2=a^2$ about the $z$ axis is
$$
\mathbf{r}(u,v) = (a\cos u)\mathbf{i} + (a\sin u)\mathbf{j} + v\mathbf{k}.
$$
Standard parametric representation of a cylinder $x^2+z^2=a^2$ about the $y$ axis is

$$
\mathbf{r}(u,v) = (a \cos{u})\mathbf{i} + v \mathbf{j} + (a\sin{u} )\mathbf{k}
$$

Standard parametric representation of a cylinder $y^2+z^2=a^2$ about the $y$ axis is

$$
\mathbf{r}(u,v) = (v)\mathbf{i} + a \cos{u} \mathbf{j} + (a\sin{u} )\mathbf{k}
$$
# [[Tangent Planes]] and Normal [[Vectors]]

## Normal Vector to a Parametric Surface

$$
	\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{r}_{u}\times \mathbf{r}_{v}
$$
Where
$\mathbf{r}_{u}=\frac{ \partial   }{ \partial u }\mathbf{r}(u,v)$
$\mathbf{r}_{v}=\frac{ \partial  }{ \partial v }\mathbf{r}(u,v)$

## Tangent Plane to a Parametric Surface

Given that equation of plane is 
$$
	(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{0})\cdot \mathbf{n}=0
$$
Equation of tangent plane to a parametric surface is given by
$$
	(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{0})\cdot(\mathbf{r}_{u}\times \mathbf{r}_{v})=0

$$
Where
$\mathbf{r}_{0}$ is the position vector of any point on $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$

# [[Surface Integral|Surface Integrals]]
## [[Surface Integral]] for Scalar Functions

$$
	\iint_{S}f(x,y,z)dS=\iint_{D}f(\mathbf{r}(u,v))||\mathbf{r}_{u}\times \mathbf{r}_{v}||dA
$$
Where
$D$ can be described with ranges of $u$ and $v$

## [[Surface Integral]] of Vector Fields
 $$
\iint _{S}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}=\iint_{D}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u,v))\cdot (\mathbf{r}_{u}\cdot \mathbf{r}_{v})dA
 $$
 Where
 The domain $D$ is a projection of the surface $S$ on the $xy$ plane

## Orientation of Surface

For surface integral of a vector field the value depends on the choice of normal vector $\mathbf{n}$

Where

$$
	\mathbf{r}_{u}\times \mathbf{r}_{v}=-\mathbf{r}_{v}\times \mathbf{r}_{u}
$$
Therefore
$$
	\iint_{-S}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}=-\iint_{S}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}

$$
# Del Operator

The del operator $\nabla$ is given by
$$
	\nabla=\frac{ \partial   }{ \partial x } \mathbf{i}+\frac{ \partial   }{ \partial y } \mathbf{j}+\frac{ \partial   }{ \partial z }\mathbf{k}
$$
## [[Curl and Divergence]]

The curl of vector function $\mathbf{F}=P\mathbf{i}+Q\mathbf{j}+R\mathbf{k}$ is defined by
$$
	\text{curl}\mathbf{F}=\nabla \times \mathbf{F}\
$$
The divergence of vector function $\mathbf{F}=P\mathbf{i}+Q\mathbf{j}+R\mathbf{k}$ is defined by
$$
	\text{div}\mathbf{F}=\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}

$$
### Curl and Conservative Field

If $\text{curl}\mathbf{F}=0$, then $\mathbf{F}$ is a conservative field

# [[Stokes Theorem]]

Let $S$ be an oriented piecewise-smooth surface that is bounded by a closed, piecewise-smooth boundary $C$.

$$
	\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}= \iint_{S}(\text{curl}\mathbf{F})\cdot d\mathbf{S}
$$
Note: Orientation of $C$ must be consistent with that of $S$.

# [[Divergence Theorem]]

Let $E$ be a solid region and let $S$ be the boundary of $E$, given with the outwards orientation

$$
	\iint_{S}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}=\iiint_{E}\text{div}\mathbf{F}\ dV
$$

Note: The outwards orientation of the boundary surface of a solid region $E$ is the one for which the normal vector points outwards
