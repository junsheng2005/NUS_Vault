# ME2102 — Lecture 08: Conventions & Symmetry

> [!summary] Chapter overview
> This lecture introduces drawing **conventions** that allow engineering drawings to communicate repeated, symmetric, small, or standard features more clearly and efficiently. The main topics are **mirror symmetry**, **aligned, enlarged and interrupted views**, **repeated elements**, conventional representations for **knurling, square-ended shafts, bearings, compression springs and gears**, and **common engineering abbreviations**.

---

## 1. Mirror Symmetry

### Symmetry line
Many parts are **mirror symmetric** and can therefore be fully represented using a **half view**.

A symmetry line is indicated by:

- a **centre line** along the axis of symmetry;
- **two short parallel lines** at each end of the centre line;
- part outlines extending **slightly beyond the centre line**.

The convention can represent symmetry about **one axis** or **two axes**.

![[Attachments/Symmetry_about_one_and_two_axes.png]]

> [!important]
> A half view represents the omitted half only when the part is symmetric about the indicated symmetry line.

### Asymmetrical features on a symmetric part
A mostly symmetric part can still contain a feature that exists on **one side only**. Such a feature must be explicitly identified rather than assumed to be mirrored.

The lecture example labels a hole with **"Hole ϕ10 — This side only"**.

![[Attachments/Symmetry_with_asymmetrical_feature.png]]

## 2. Special Views

### 2.1 Aligned views
An ordinary projected view can sometimes give the impression that a part is **asymmetric**, especially when repeated features are arranged around a circular part.

An **aligned view** rotates or aligns the relevant features into the viewing plane so that their arrangement can be represented more clearly.

The lecture notes that an aligned view is **often shown without added indication**.

![[Attachments/Aligned_view_hole_pattern.png]]

### 2.2 Enlarged part views
Some components contain features that are **too small to dimension clearly at the general drawing scale**.

For these cases:

1. Enclose the small feature using a **thin circle**.
2. Draw a **leader** from the circled region.
3. Show the selected region as an **enlarged part view** so that it can be dimensioned clearly.

![[Attachments/Enlarged_part_view.png]]

### 2.3 Interrupted views
Long parts of **constant cross-section** may be shortened using an interrupted view.

The convention represents the part as though:

1. a portion from the centre has been removed; and
2. the two remaining ends have been brought together.

The lecture shows interrupted-view conventions for:

- **circular sections**;
- **tubes**;
- **rectangular sections**.

![[Attachments/Interrupted_views.png]]

> [!important]
> The interrupted view shortens the drawing representation of a long constant-section part; it does not imply that the actual component contains the break shown in the drawing.

## 3. Repetitive Information

When identical parts or features occur many times, drawing every repeated feature may be unnecessary.

Instead:

- draw **one** representative part or feature; and
- indicate the positions of the remaining identical features using their **centre lines**.

![[Attachments/Repetitive_information_centrelines.png]]

For repeated parts arranged in a **regular pattern**, the same principle is used to avoid unnecessary repeated detail while preserving the locations of the repeated elements.

![[Attachments/Repeated_parts_regular_pattern.png]]

## 4. Part Conventions

### 4.1 Knurling
The lecture introduces two types of knurling:

- **straight knurling**;
- **diamond knurling**.

Their conventional representations use **thin lines**.

Straight knurling is noted as being often used on the heads of **hexagon socket cap screws**.

![[Attachments/Straight_and_diamond_knurling.png]]

> [!example] Recognition
> Parallel straight lines indicate **straight knurling**, while crossed diagonal lines indicate **diamond knurling**.

### 4.2 Square-ended shafts
A **square end on a shaft** is represented by **diagonals drawn on the flat face**.

![[Attachments/Square_end_shaft_convention.png]]

### 4.3 Bearings
A similar crossed-box convention is used for **ball and roller bearings**.

![[Attachments/Bearing_convention.png]]

> [!important]
> In the lecture practice question, the crossed boxes in the shaft drawing represent a **bearing**.

### 4.4 Cylindrical compression springs
For the conventional representation of a cylindrical compression spring:

- the helices should **not be projected as true helices**;
- they are drawn as **straight lines**;
- the pitch does **not need to be exact**;
- **2 or 3 coils** should be shown at each end.

For diagrams and schematic drawings, the spring may be simplified further to a **single-line representation**.

![[Attachments/Compression_spring_convention.png]]

### 4.5 Gears
For the circular view of a gear:

- individual teeth may be **omitted**;
- a **thick circle** represents the **tips of the teeth**;
- a **thin chain circle** represents the **pitch circle**.

The lecture also illustrates common gear forms including **spur**, **helical**, **straight tooth bevelled**, **worm gear**, **worm**, **pinion**, and **rack**.

For a rack:

- one or two teeth are drawn;
- a **thick line** represents the tooth tips;
- a **thin line** represents the roots;
- the **pitch line is not shown**.

![[Attachments/Gear_and_rack_conventions.png]]

## Chapter Summary / Key Connections

Engineering drawing conventions reduce unnecessary drawing detail while preserving the information needed to interpret a component correctly.

- **Symmetry lines** allow symmetric components to be represented using partial views.
- **Aligned views** prevent circularly arranged features from appearing misleadingly asymmetric.
- **Enlarged part views** make small features easier to dimension.
- **Interrupted views** shorten the representation of long constant-section parts.
- **Repetitive-information conventions** avoid repeatedly drawing identical features.
- Standard conventions allow **knurling, square ends, bearings, springs, gears and racks** to be represented efficiently.
- Standard **abbreviations** further reduce drawing clutter while retaining clear meaning.

## Key Concepts / Frameworks

> [!summary] Core conventions
> - **Symmetry:** centre line with parallel end marks; part lines extend beyond the centre line.
> - **Aligned view:** aligns features to communicate their arrangement clearly.
> - **Enlarged part view:** enlarges a small circled feature for clear dimensioning.
> - **Interrupted view:** shortens a long constant-cross-section part in the drawing.
> - **Repetitive information:** draw one identical feature and locate repetitions with centre lines.
> - **Knurling:** thin-line representation; straight or diamond.
> - **Square end:** diagonals on the flat face.
> - **Bearing:** crossed-box conventional representation.
> - **Compression spring:** helices represented using straight lines; 2 or 3 coils shown at each end.
> - **Gear:** thick tip circle and thin chain pitch circle; teeth may be omitted in circular view.
> - **Rack:** one or two teeth shown; thick tip line, thin root line, no pitch line.
