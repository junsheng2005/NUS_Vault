## 1. Value Proposition Canvas  
  
A strong value proposition requires a clear ****fit**** between the customer profile and the value map.  
  
### Customer Profile  
  
- ****Customer jobs:**** What customers are trying to accomplish.  
- ****Pains:**** The difficulties, costs, risks, and frustrations they experience.  
- ****Gains:**** The outcomes and benefits they want.  
  
### Value Map  
  
- ****Products and services:**** What the business provides.  
- ****Pain relievers:**** How the offering reduces customer difficulties.  
- ****Gain creators:**** How the offering produces desired outcomes.  
  
The process should begin with the customer's desired outcome—not with product features. Teams should prioritise the most important jobs, pains, and gains instead of attempting to address everything.  
  
## 2. Hilti Tool Fleet Management Case  
  
Hilti manufactures high-quality power tools. Because its tools last a long time, customers do not replace them frequently. Hilti therefore needed a new way to generate growth.  
  
### Target Customer  
  
The target customers were CEOs of construction companies. Their real job was not merely to purchase drills. They needed to complete profitable contracts by having the right tools at the right place and time.  
  
### Customer Jobs and Gains  
  
- Accept and execute profitable construction contracts.  
- Maintain high tool availability and minimise downtime.  
- Plan the allocation of tools across different worksites.  
- Keep costs predictable.  
- Improve productivity and worker safety.  
- Complete projects reliably and avoid penalties.  
  
### Customer Pains  
  
- Heavy upfront investment in tools.  
- Broken, missing, or stolen equipment.  
- Repair and replacement delays.  
- Project delays and contractual penalties.  
- Difficulty changing tool capacity when workloads fluctuate.  
- Administrative effort required to manage the tool fleet.  
  
### Hilti's Value Proposition  
  
Hilti introduced a subscription-based fleet-management service that provided:  
  
- Tools for a regular monthly fee.  
- Inventory tracking through labels and RFID.  
- Rapid repair or replacement at no additional charge.  
- Regular upgrades to newer tools.  
- Tools on demand during peak periods.  
- A single, predictable invoice.  
  
This transformed Hilti from a company that merely sold tools into a provider of productivity and tool availability. Each part of the service directly addressed a customer pain or created a desired gain.  
  
The lecturer reported that this model greatly increased customer loyalty and expanded into numerous international markets.  
  
## 3. Value Proposition Statement  
  
A ****Value Proposition Statement (VPS)**** is a concise written expression of the value proposition. It should identify:  
  
- The intended customer.  
- The customer's problem or unmet need.  
- The product or service being offered.  
- Its principal benefit.  
- Why it is better than the available alternatives.  
  
### Possible Formats  
  
> We provide ****[customer]**** with ****[offering]**** so that they can achieve ****[benefit]****.  
  
Alternatively:  
  
> For ****[customer]**** who is dissatisfied with ****[existing alternative]****, our ****[product]**** provides ****[benefit]****, unlike ****[competitor or alternative]****.  
  
The VPS and Value Proposition Canvas should communicate the same underlying value. If they contradict one another, the proposition is probably insufficiently clear.  
  
### Characteristics of a Good Value Proposition  
  
A good value proposition should be:  
  
- Targeted at a clearly defined customer.  
- Authentic and achievable.  
- Clearly differentiated from alternatives.  
- Easy to understand.  
- Based on clear and realisable benefits.  
- Valuable enough that customers are willing to pay for it.  
  
## 4. Netflix Example  
  
Netflix's delivery system evolved through several stages:  
  
1. DVDs delivered by mail.  
2. Online streaming.  
3. Original content production.  
  
Despite these changes, its underlying value proposition remained broadly consistent:  
  
- Convenient access to a large selection of entertainment.  
- A predictable subscription fee.  
- Customer control over what and when to watch.  
  
The main lesson is that the technology, activities, and value-delivery system can change while the core customer value remains durable.  
  
## 5. Stakeholder Analysis  
  
A stakeholder is anyone who affects, is affected by, or cares about a project. Stakeholders can include:  
  
- Customers and users.  
- Investors and sponsors.  
- Advisors.  
- Employees and project partners.  
- Regulators and government agencies.  
- Groups that support or oppose the project.  
  
Both positive and negative interest count. Someone who opposes the project may still have significant influence over its success.  
  
### Power–Interest Matrix  
  
| Power | Interest | Recommended Treatment |  
|---|---|---|  
| High | High | Manage closely; these are the key players. |  
| High | Low | Keep satisfied. |  
| Low | High | Keep informed. |  
| Low | Low | Monitor with minimal effort. |  
  
The stakeholder map is dynamic. A stakeholder with little present interest may become highly involved when a problem or complaint arises. Teams should therefore revisit the map as circumstances change.  
  
### Different Customer Roles  
  
The following roles may be held by different people:  
  
- ****Decision-maker:**** Chooses whether the product will be adopted.  
- ****Economic buyer:**** Pays for the product.  
- ****Customer:**** Receives the offering or its value.  
- ****End user:**** Actually uses the product.  
  
For example, a baby uses a diaper, but a parent chooses and purchases it. Teams must determine whose needs and influence should shape the offering.
