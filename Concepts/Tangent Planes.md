# Vector Calculus Notes: Tangent Planes

**Prerequisite Concept: The Tangent Line**

In standard calculus, a tangent line "just touches" a 2D curve at a specific point. It represents the best linear (straight-line) approximation of the curve near that point. The slope of the tangent line is given by the derivative.

## 1. The Core Concept

A **tangent plane** is the 3D equivalent of a tangent line. If you zoom in closely enough on a smooth 3D surface at a specific point, the surface will look flat. A tangent plane is the perfectly flat plane that "just touches" the surface at that point.

Crucially, the tangent plane at a given point contains _all_ the possible tangent lines to the surface at that point.

To define any plane in 3D space, you need two things:

1. **A point** on the plane $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$.
    
2. **A normal vector** $\mathbf{n} = \langle A, B, C \rangle$ that is perfectly perpendicular to the plane.
    

How we find that normal vector depends on how the surface is mathematically defined.

## 2. Tangent Planes for Explicit Surfaces ($z = f(x,y)$)

If your surface is defined as a function where $z$ is explicitly isolated, you find the slopes of the surface in the x-direction and y-direction using **partial derivatives**.

- $f_x(x_0, y_0)$ is the slope of the surface in the x-direction.
    
- $f_y(x_0, y_0)$ is the slope of the surface in the y-direction.
    

### The Mathematics

The equation of the tangent plane to the surface $z = f(x,y)$ at the point $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ is:

$$z - z_0 = f_x(x_0, y_0)(x - x_0) + f_y(x_0, y_0)(y - y_0)$$

_(Notice how similar this is to the point-slope form of a 2D line: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$)_

### Example 1: The Paraboloid

Find the equation of the tangent plane to the paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2$ at the point $(1, 2, 5)$.

1. **Find the partial derivatives:**
    
    - $f_x(x, y) = 2x$
        
    - $f_y(x, y) = 2y$
        
2. **Evaluate them at the point $(1, 2)$:**
    
    - $f_x(1, 2) = 2(1) = 2$
        
    - $f_y(1, 2) = 2(2) = 4$
        
3. **Plug everything into the tangent plane equation:**
    
    $$z - 5 = 2(x - 1) + 4(y - 2)$$
    
4. **Simplify (optional but common):**
    
    $$z - 5 = 2x - 2 + 4y - 8$$
    
    $$2x + 4y - z = 5$$
    

## 3. Tangent Planes for Implicit Surfaces ($F(x,y,z) = k$)

Often, surfaces are represented as "level surfaces" where all variables are mixed together and set equal to a constant $k$. A sphere is a great example.

For implicit surfaces, we use the **gradient** ($\nabla F$). The gradient vector of a function is a beautiful mathematical tool because it _always points perpendicularly away from a level surface_. Therefore, the gradient at our specific point gives us our exact normal vector $\mathbf{n}$!

### The Mathematics

If a surface is defined by $F(x,y,z) = k$, the normal vector at point $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ is $\nabla F(x_0, y_0, z_0) = \langle F_x, F_y, F_z \rangle$.

The equation of the tangent plane is:

$$F_x(x_0, y_0, z_0)(x - x_0) + F_y(x_0, y_0, z_0)(y - y_0) + F_z(x_0, y_0, z_0)(z - z_0) = 0$$

### Example 2: The Sphere

Find the equation of the tangent plane to the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 14$ at the point $(1, 2, 3)$.

1. **Define $F(x,y,z)$ and find the gradient:**
    
    - $F(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$
        
    - $\nabla F = \langle 2x, 2y, 2z \rangle$
        
2. **Evaluate the gradient at the point $(1, 2, 3)$ to get the normal vector:**
    
    - $\nabla F(1, 2, 3) = \langle 2(1), 2(2), 2(3) \rangle = \langle 2, 4, 6 \rangle$
        
3. **Plug the normal vector and point into the plane equation:**
    
    $$2(x - 1) + 4(y - 2) + 6(z - 3) = 0$$
    
4. **Simplify:**
    
    $$2x - 2 + 4y - 8 + 6z - 18 = 0$$
    
    $$2x + 4y + 6z = 28$$
    
    _(Dividing by 2)_
    
    $$x + 2y + 3z = 14$$

## 4. Tangent Planes for Parametric Surfaces ($\mathbf{r}(u,v)$)

When a surface is defined parametrically by a vector function $\mathbf{r}(u,v) = \langle x(u,v), y(u,v), z(u,v) \rangle$, you cannot use simple partial derivatives or gradients directly on $x, y, z$.

Instead, you use the partial derivatives of the vector function itself to find two distinct tangent vectors that lie perfectly on the surface. Taking their cross product gives you the normal vector required to build the plane.

### The Mathematics

Given a parametric surface $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ and a specific point on the surface determined by the parameter values $(u_0, v_0)$:

1. **Find the two tangent vectors** by taking partial derivatives:
    
    - $\mathbf{r}_u = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u} = \langle \frac{\partial x}{\partial u}, \frac{\partial y}{\partial u}, \frac{\partial z}{\partial u} \rangle$ (Tangent vector along the $u$-grid curve)
        
    - $\mathbf{r}_v = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v} = \langle \frac{\partial x}{\partial v}, \frac{\partial y}{\partial v}, \frac{\partial z}{\partial v} \rangle$ (Tangent vector along the $v$-grid curve)
        
2. **Find the normal vector ($\mathbf{n}$)** by taking their cross product at $(u_0, v_0)$:
    
    $$\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{r}_u(u_0, v_0) \times \mathbf{r}_v(u_0, v_0) = \langle A, B, C \rangle$$
    
3. **Write the standard plane equation** using the components of $\mathbf{n}$ and the initial point $\mathbf{r}(u_0, v_0) = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$:
    
    $$A(x - x_0) + B(y - y_0) + C(z - z_0) = 0$$
    

> **Note:** If $\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v = \mathbf{0}$, the surface is not "smooth" at that point (like the tip of a cone), and a unique tangent plane does not exist.

### Example 3: The Parametric Cylinder

Find the equation of the tangent plane to the parametric cylinder $\mathbf{r}(u,v) = \langle 2\cos(u), 2\sin(u), v \rangle$ at the point where $u = \frac{\pi}{4}$ and $v = 3$.

1. **Find the base point $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$:**
    
    - $\mathbf{r}(\frac{\pi}{4}, 3) = \langle 2\cos(\frac{\pi}{4}), 2\sin(\frac{\pi}{4}), 3 \rangle = \langle \sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2}, 3 \rangle$
        
2. **Find the tangent vectors $\mathbf{r}_u$ and $\mathbf{r}_v$:**
    
    - $\mathbf{r}_u = \langle -2\sin(u), 2\cos(u), 0 \rangle$
        
    - $\mathbf{r}_v = \langle 0, 0, 1 \rangle$
        
3. **Evaluate the tangent vectors at $u = \frac{\pi}{4}, v = 3$:**
    
    - $\mathbf{r}_u(\frac{\pi}{4}, 3) = \langle -2(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}), 2(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}), 0 \rangle = \langle -\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2}, 0 \rangle$
        
    - $\mathbf{r}_v(\frac{\pi}{4}, 3) = \langle 0, 0, 1 \rangle$
        
4. **Calculate the normal vector ($\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v$):**
    
    $$\mathbf{n} = \det \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ -\sqrt{2} & \sqrt{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$
    
    $$\mathbf{n} = (\sqrt{2} - 0)\hat{i} - (-\sqrt{2} - 0)\hat{j} + (0 - 0)\hat{k} = \langle \sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2}, 0 \rangle$$
    
5. **Plug the normal vector $\langle \sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2}, 0 \rangle$ and point $(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2}, 3)$ into the plane equation:**
    
    $$\sqrt{2}(x - \sqrt{2}) + \sqrt{2}(y - \sqrt{2}) + 0(z - 3) = 0$$
    
    $$\sqrt{2}x - 2 + \sqrt{2}y - 2 = 0$$
    
    $$\sqrt{2}x + \sqrt{2}y = 4$$
    

_(Dividing everything by $\sqrt{2}$ gives the final simplified note)_

$$x + y = 2\sqrt{2}$$
