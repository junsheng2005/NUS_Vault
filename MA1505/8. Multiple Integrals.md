# [[Double Integrals]]

## Geometric Meaning

Geometrically if $f(x,y)\geq{0}$ for all $(x,y) \in R$ , the definite [[integral]] $\iint_{R} f(x,y)dA$ is equal to the volume under the surface $z=f(x,y)$ and above the $xy$ plane over the region $R$  

>[!info]- Image
>![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780899474520.webp|386x324]]

## Properties of Double Integrals
$$
	\begin{flalign*}
			\text{(1)}&\ \iint_{R}(f(x,y)+g(x,y))dA=\iint_{R}f(x,y)dA+\iint_{R}g(x,y)dA\\
			\text{(2)}& \ \iint_{R}cf(x,y)dA=c\iint_{R}f(x,y)dA,\text{Where}\ c\ \text{is a constant}	\\
			(3)&\ \text{If} \ f(x,y)\geq g(x,y)\ \text{for all}\ (x,y)\in R,\text{then}\ \iint_{R}f(x,y)dA\geq \iint_{R}g(x,y)dA	\\
			(4)&\ \iint_{R}dA=A(R), \text{the area of region} \ R\\
			(5)& \iint_{R}f(x,y)dA=\iint_{R_{1}}f(x,y)dA+\iint _{R_{2}}f(x,y)dA, \text{where} \ R=R_{1} \cup R_{2} \ \\ &\text{and}\ R_{1},R_{2} \ \text{do not overlap}\\
			(6)&\  \text{If} \ m\leq f(x,y)\leq M \ \text{for all}\ (x,y)\in R, \text{then}\ mA(R)\leq \iint_{R}f(x,y)dA\leq MA(R)
	
	\end{flalign*}
$$
## Evaluation of Double Integrals

### Rectangular Regions

Given a rectangular region $R$ in the $xy$ plane is described as,
$$
	R:a\leq x\leq b,\ c\leq y\leq d
$$
Then
$$
	\iint_{R}f(x,y)dA=\int^d_{c}\left[ \int^b_{a}f(x,y)dx \right]dy=\int^b_{a}\left[ \int^d_{c}f(x,y)dy \right]dx
$$
>[!Example]- Example
Let $R$ be the rectangular region
>
$$0 \le x \le 4, \quad 1 \le y \le 2.$$
>
Evaluate $\iint_R x^2y \, dA$.
>
**Solution:**
>
$$\begin{aligned} \iint_R x^2y \, dA &= \int_0^4 \int_1^2 x^2y \, dydx \\ &= \left( \int_0^4 x^2 \, dx \right) \left( \int_1^2 y \, dy \right) \\ &= \frac{64}{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = 32. \end{aligned}$$

## General Region Type A

Bottom and top boundaries are curves given by $y=g_{1}(x)$ and $y=g_{2}(x)$ respectively. Left and right boundaries are given by straight lines.

>[!info]- Image
>![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780965968812.webp]]

The region $R$ is given by,

$$
	R: \quad g_{1}(x)\leq y\leq g_{2}(x),\quad a\leq x\leq b.

$$
Then
$$
	\iint_{R}f(x,y)dA=\int^b_{a}\left[ \int^{g_{2}(x)}_{g_{1}(x)}f(x,y)dy \right]dx

$$

## General Region Type B

Left and right are curves given by $x=g_{1}(y)$ and $x=g_{2}(y)$ respectively. Top and bottom boundaries are given by straight lines.

>[!info]- Image
>![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780966325691.webp]]

The region $R$ is given by,

$$
	R: \quad g_{1}(y)\leq x\leq g_{2}(y),\quad c\leq y\leq d.

$$
Then
$$
	\iint_{R}f(x,y)dA=\int^d_{c}\left[ \int^{g_{2}(y)}_{g_{1}(y)}f(x,y)dx \right]dy

$$
>[!example]- Example 1
>If $R$ is bounded by $y = x$ and $y = x^2$, find $\iint_R 30xy \, dA$.
>
**Solution:** Treat $R$ as a Type A region:
>
$$R : \quad x^2 \le y \le x, \quad 0 \le x \le 1$$
>
![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780966575560.webp|349x274]]
>
Then
>
$$\begin{aligned} \iint_R 30xy \, dA &= \int_0^1 \left[ \int_{x^2}^x 30xy \, dy \right] dx \\ &= \int_0^1 \left[ 15xy^2 \right]_{y=x^2}^{y=x} dx = \int_0^1 15x(x^2 - x^4) \, dx \\ &= \left[ \frac{15x^4}{4} - \frac{15x^6}{6} \right]_{x=0}^{x=1} = \frac{5}{4}. \end{aligned}$$
>
If we treat $R$ as a type B region:
>
$$R : \quad y \le x \le \sqrt{y}, \quad 0 \le y \le 1$$
>
![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780966590497.webp|361x267]]
>
$$\begin{aligned} \iint_R 30xy \, dA &= \int_0^1 \left[ \int_y^{\sqrt{y}} 30xy \, dx \right] dy \\ &= \int_0^1 \left[ 15x^2y \right]_{x=y}^{x=\sqrt{y}} dy = \int_0^1 (15y^2 - 15y^3) \, dy \\ &= \left[ \frac{15y^3}{3} - \frac{15y^4}{4} \right]_{y=0}^{y=1} = \frac{5}{4}. \end{aligned}$$

>[!example]- Example 2
>Calculate $\iint_R \frac{\sin x}{x} \, dA$, where $R$ is the triangle in the $xy$-plane bounded by the $x$-axis, the line $y = x$ and the line $x = 1$.
>
**Solution:** If we treat $R$ as type B:
>
$$R : \quad y \le x \le 1, \quad 0 \le y \le 1.$$
>
![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780966760270.webp|333x248]]
>
$$\iint_R \frac{\sin x}{x} \, dA = \int_0^1 \left[ \int_y^1 \frac{\sin x}{x} \, dx \right] dy,$$
>
which cannot be evaluated by elementary means.
>
If we treat $R$ as type A:
>
$$R : \quad 0 \le y \le x, \quad 0 \le x \le 1.$$
>
![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780966772195.webp|330x268]]
>
$$\begin{aligned} \iint_R \frac{\sin x}{x} \, dA &= \int_0^1 \left[ \int_0^x \frac{\sin x}{x} \, dy \right] dx \\ &= \int_0^1 \left[ y \frac{\sin x}{x} \right]_{y=0}^{y=x} dx = \int_0^1 (\sin x - 0) \, dx \end{aligned}$$

>[!example]- Example 3
>Evaluate $\int_0^3 \int_{\sqrt{x/3}}^1 e^{y^3} \, dy \, dx$.
>
**Solution:** $R$ may be described as:
>
$$R : \quad \sqrt{\frac{x}{3}} \le y \le 1, \quad 0 \le x \le 3.$$
>
Hence the Type A region looks like
>
![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780966918752.webp|416]]
>
However, it is difficult to integrate $e^{y^3}$ directly.
>
Now we treat $R$ as type B:
>
Note that $y = \sqrt{\frac{x}{3}} \implies x = 3y^2$.
>
So the region $R$ is given by
>
$$R : \quad 0 \le x \le 3y^2, \quad 0 \le y \le 1.$$
>
![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780966934413.webp|408]]
>
$$\begin{aligned} \int_0^3 \int_{\sqrt{x/3}}^1 e^{y^3} \, dy \, dx &= \int_0^1 \left[ \int_0^{3y^2} e^{y^3} \, dx \right] dy \\ &= \int_0^1 \left[ xe^{y^3} \right]_{x=0}^{x=3y^2} dy = \int_0^1 3y^2 e^{y^3} \, dy \\ &= \int_0^1 e^u \, du = \left[ e^u \right]_{u=0}^{u=1} = e - 1. \end{aligned}$$
>
(Here we have used a substitution $u = y^3$.)

# Double Integral in Polar Coordinates

## Change of Variables

When transforming from cartesian coordinates to polar coordinates, the variables change from $(x,y)$ to $(r,\theta)$:
$$
	x=r\cos\theta, \quad y=r\sin\theta

	$$
In this case $dA$ will be change from $dxdy$ to $r\ drd\theta$

Suppose $R$ is given in polar coordinates
$$
	R:\quad a\leq r\leq b,\quad \alpha\leq\theta\leq\beta,
$$
Then,
$$
		\iint_{R}f(x,y)dA=\int^\beta _{\alpha} \int^b _{a} f(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)r\ drd\theta

$$
## Circle

![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780967084992.webp]]

$$
	R:\quad 0\leq r\leq1, \quad 0\leq\theta\leq2\pi
$$

## Example

>[!example]- Example 1
>Evaluate $\iint_R (3x + 4y^2) \, dA$, where $R$ is the semicircular ring in the upper half-plane between the semicircles $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ and $x^2 + y^2 = 4$.
>
![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780982266267.webp|329x199]]__
>
**Solution:** The region $R$ is given by
>
$$R : \quad 1 \le r \le 2, \quad 0 \le \theta \le \pi.$$
>
So
>
$$\begin{aligned} \iint_R (3x + 4y^2) \, dA &= \int_0^\pi \int_1^2 (3r\cos\theta + 4r^2\sin^2\theta) \, r \, drd\theta \\ &= \int_0^\pi \left[ r^3\cos\theta + r^4\sin^2\theta \right]_{r=1}^{r=2} d\theta \\ &= \int_0^\pi (7\cos\theta + 15\sin^2\theta) \, d\theta \\ &= \int_0^\pi \left( 7\cos\theta + \frac{15}{2}(1 - \cos 2\theta) \right) d\theta \\ &= \left[ 7\sin\theta + \frac{15}{2}\left(\theta - \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2}\right) \right]_{\theta=0}^{\theta=\pi} \\ &= \frac{15\pi}{2} \end{aligned}$$

>[!example]- Example 2
>**(a) Find the equation of the tangent line to the polar graph**
>
$$r=\theta,$$
>
where
>
$$0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi$$
>![[8. Multiple Integrals-1781006694728.webp|292x266]]
Converting Cartesian coordinates to polar coordinates:
>
$$x=r\cos\theta,\quad y=r\sin\theta$$
>
Using chain rule:
>
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy}{d\theta}\times \frac{d\theta}{dx}$$
>
So,
>
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{dy}{d\theta}&= r\cos\theta+\frac{dr}{d\theta}\sin\theta \\ \frac{dx}{d\theta}&= -r\sin\theta+\frac{dr}{d\theta}\cos\theta\\ \therefore \frac{dy}{dx}&=\frac{r\cos\theta+\sin\theta}{\cos\theta-r\sin\theta},\quad \left( \frac{dr}{d\theta}=1 \right) \end{aligned}$$
>
 **(b) Find the area of the graph in (a)**
>
$$\text{Area} = \iint_{R}dA$$
>
$$R:\quad 0\leq r\leq\theta,\quad 0\leq\theta\leq2\pi$$
>
$$\therefore \text{Area}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^\theta r\ drd\theta=\frac{4\pi^3}{3}$$

# Application of Double Integrals

## Volume

Suppose $D$ is a solid region under the surface of a function $f(x,y)$ over a plane region $R$. Then the volume of $D$ is given by,
$$
	\iint_{R}f(x,y)dA

$$
>[!example]- Example 1
>Find the volume of the solid $D$ that is bounded by the elliptic paraboloid $x^2 + 2y^2 + z = 16$, the planes $x = 2$, $y = 2$, and the 3-coordinate planes.
>
**Solution:** The solid region $D$ is under the surface represented by the function $f(x,y) = 16 - x^2 - 2y^2$ and is above the rectangular region
>
$$R : \quad 0 \le x \le 2, \quad 0 \le y \le 2.$$
![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780982518470.webp]]
>
So volume of $D$ is
>
$$\begin{aligned} &\iint_R (16 - x^2 - 2y^2) \, dA \\ &= \int_0^2 \int_0^2 (16 - x^2 - 2y^2) \, dxdy \\ &= 48. \end{aligned}$$

>[!example]- Example 2
>
Find the volume of the solid enclosed laterally by the circular cylinder about $z$-axis of radius 3 and bounded on top by the plane $x + z = 20$ and below by the paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2$.
>
**Solution:** The volume can be computed as
>
$$V = \iint_R f_1(x,y) \, dA - \iint_R f_2(x,y) \, dA$$
>
where $f_1$ and $f_2$ are the functions of the top and bottom surfaces respectively and $R$ is the projection of the solid on the $xy$-plane.
>
From the equations of the plane and paraboloid, we have
>
$$f_1(x,y) = 20 - x \quad \text{and} \quad f_2(x,y) = x^2 + y^2.$$
>
It is also clear that $R$ is the circle of radius 3 centred at origin which has polar coordinates
>
$$0 \le r \le 3, \quad 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi.$$
>
So the volume of the solid is
>
$$V = \iint_R (20 - x) \, dA - \iint_R (x^2 + y^2) \, dA$$
>
and can be computed as
>
$$\begin{aligned} V &= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^3 (20 - r\cos\theta)r \, dr d\theta - \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^3 (r^2)r \, dr d\theta \\ &= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^3 (20r - r^2\cos\theta - r^3) \, dr d\theta \\ &= \int_0^{2\pi} \left[ 10r^2 - \frac{r^3}{3}\cos\theta - \frac{r^4}{4} \right]_0^3 d\theta \\ &= \int_0^{2\pi} \left( 90 - 9\cos\theta - \frac{81}{4} \right) d\theta \\ &= \left[ \frac{279}{4}\theta - 9\sin\theta \right]_0^{2\pi} = \frac{279}{2}\pi \end{aligned}$$

## Surface Area

If $f$ has a continuous first partial differentiation on a closed region $R$ of the $xy$ plane, then the area $S$ of that portion of the surface $z=f(x,y)$ that projects onto $R$ is,

$$
	S=\iint_{R}\sqrt{ \left( \frac{ \partial z }{ \partial x }  \right)^2 +\left( \frac{ \partial z }{ \partial y }  \right)^2 +1}\ dA
$$

>[!example]- Example
>Find the surface area of the portion of the cylinder $x^2 + z^2 = 4$ above the rectangle
>
$$R : \quad 0 \le x \le 1, \quad 0 \le y \le 4.$$
>
![[8. Multiple Integrals-1780982873022.webp|359x232]]
>
**Solution:** The portion of the cylinder $x^2 + z^2 = 4$ that lies above the $xy$-plane has the equation $z = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$.
>
So the surface is given by the function $f(x,y) = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$.
>
$$\begin{aligned} S &= \iint_R \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right)^2 + 1} \, dA \\ &= \iint_R \sqrt{\left(-\frac{x}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}\right)^2 + 0^2 + 1} \, dA \\ &= \int_0^4 \left[ \int_0^1 \frac{2}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}} \, dx \right] dy \\ &= 2 \int_0^4 \left[ \sin^{-1}(x/2) \right]_{x=0}^{x=1} dy \\ &= 2 \int_0^4 \frac{\pi}{6} \, dy = \frac{4\pi}{3}. \end{aligned}$$
>
Note that $\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}} \, dx = \sin^{-1}(x/a) + C$.

## Average Value of a Function

The average value of a function of two variable $f(x,y)$ over region $R$ is given by,

$$
	\frac{1}{\text{Area of}\ R}\iint_{R}f(x,y)dA
$$
